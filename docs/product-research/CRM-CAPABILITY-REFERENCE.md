## Capability groups

### 1. CRM consulting and business analysis

- Map the actual sales pipeline, not a generic stage list
- Map customer lifecycle and internal workflows
- Define reporting needs before building screens
- Produce requirements for roles, workflows, reports, and integrations
- Draft a data model: entities, fields, relationships, owners
- Define KPIs for pipeline health and conversion
- Produce a prioritised backlog with acceptance criteria

### 2. Custom CRM development

- Pipelines, stages, tasks, and forecasting that match the sales process
- Role-based UI for daily work
- Marketing automation rules for routing and follow-ups
- Mobile and desktop UI
- Architecture that can grow with users and record volume

### 3. CRM customisation

- Custom objects, fields, and relationships
- Automations and validations that keep data clean
- UI and dashboards adjusted per team
- Changes that remain maintainable
- Configuration of an existing process rather than a new platform

### 4. CRM integrations

- Source-of-truth rules per data field
- Reliable sync: retries, error handling, monitoring
- One consistent view of leads, opportunities, and customers
- Reduce manual re-entry and duplicates
- Connect CRM to ERP, accounting, marketing, support, and warehouses

### 5. CRM data migration

- Data profiling before migration
- Cleanup and deduplication rules
- Field mapping and test migrations
- Validation checks before cutover
- Reconciliation report with exceptions

### 6. CRM support and scaling

- Support with explicit SLAs
- Performance tuning as usage grows
- Feature updates on a stable release cadence
- Role-based access and audit logs
- Data-protection controls suitable for audits

### 7. Sales and opportunity management

- Lead tracking with clear ownership
- Deal stages with required fields
- Tasks and reminders
- Forecasting as a process, not a spreadsheet cell
- Rules that prevent ambiguous pipeline numbers

### 8. Customer relationship management

- Customer profiles, interactions, and history
- Segmentation in the data model
- Handoffs between sales, support, and account management
- Duplicate reduction

### 9. Marketing automation

- Campaigns, triggers, scoring, and attribution
- Explainable, maintainable rules
- Repeatable follow-ups and routing
- Visible lead quality for sales handoff

### 10. Analytics and reporting

- KPIs around pipeline definitions
- Conversion by stage, velocity, source performance, activity
- Stable reporting when a team changes a process
- Spot where revenue leaks

### 11. Workflow automation

- Approvals
- Notifications
- Task automation
- Validation rules
- Prevent silent process drift

### 12. Security, access, and compliance

- Role-based access
- Audit logs
- Export and sensitive-field controls
- Traceability for audits and investigations
- Data-handling rules that can be shown, not a checklist

## Process capabilities (not a delivery vendor plan)

These are product capabilities Flow already expresses as discovery,
configuration, and activation — not a client-services statement of work.

- Discovery: workflow map, requirements, data-model draft, access model
- Surfaces: screens around daily actions
- Integration: field mapping, ownership, sync, retries, logs
- Validation: counts, mapping checks, duplicates, permission review
- Cutover: controlled activation, training artefacts, post-launch backlog
